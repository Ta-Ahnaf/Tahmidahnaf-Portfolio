Project summary — The Mind Detective

After finishing a machine learning course, I wanted to go deeper than the coursework — that's where the idea for an emotion detector came from. I chose the GoEmotions dataset as the foundation, a rich set of 27 emotions plus neutral, and trained a DistilBERT model to classify emotion from text, saving the model and tokenizer with Hugging Face's save_pretrained.

The backend — data prep, training, saving the model — was built entirely from scratch. For the frontend, I leaned heavily on ChatGPT to speed things up and polish the interface. Once deployed on Streamlit, the first predictions came back as raw class numbers like class_9; mapping those back to real emotion names let the app finally speak plain language: "You are feeling joy."

The result is a clean, simple web app that reads text, understands it through a trained model, and responds with the most likely emotion behind it — a mix of real backend work and some handy AI-powered assistance on the front end.

Notebook margins

Things I've written down

Do not be sorry, be better

Kratos said this once, and it captures exactly how I feel about the word "sorry." If you've messed something up in my life, don't drown me in apologies — fix it, or leave me the space to fix it myself. Repeating "sorry" only keeps the mistake alive in my head. What I actually need is peace, and the room to heal in my own way — forgiveness only becomes real once I feel it, not once it's been said enough times.

Don't set a destination, set a direction

If I say I want to reach Amsterdam, what happens the moment I get there? Instead, pick a direction — let your goal be moving west simply because you love going west. Every step toward it becomes a success on its own, and even after "arriving" there's always more west to explore. Love the journey, not just the destination, and you'll find better experiences — and better company — along the way.

Video games are the best storytelling medium right now

Fiction has always been about the experience. When film first showed audiences a train pulling into a station, people reportedly panicked — a level of immersion books alone couldn't touch. For a long time film held that crown. Now I think games have taken it: they don't just tell you a story, they let you live it, choose it, and shape how it ends.

You don't need to be serious

Discipline needs seriousness — the maturity to keep emotion from derailing a goal. But when your emotions actually support the goal you're chasing, seriousness quietly disappears. You stop forcing it. You think about it all day, do it without hesitation, and genuinely enjoy every part of it. That's when passion turns effortless.

Horses over dogs

Dogs are amazing — genuinely my third favourite animal — but horses take the top spot for me. They've carried people into war, stood on the battlefield, and helped bring the injured home. The trust built with a horse in a life-and-death moment sits on a different level entirely. Mostly, though, I just love horses. No further logic required.
